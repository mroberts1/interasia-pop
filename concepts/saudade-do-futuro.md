# Saudade do Futuro

**Saudade do futuro** (Portuguese: "nostalgia for the future") is an affective concept describing a paradoxical yearning for a promised future that never materialized or for futures imagined in the past that now seem unattainable. Musicologist [[Martin Roberts]] argues this concept captures the emotional and aesthetic core of [[Shibuya-kei]] music and 1990s postmodern culture more broadly.

## The Concept

Saudade do futuro combines two ideas:

### [[Saudade]]
Portuguese concept of profound, bittersweet nostalgia for a person, place, time, or memory from which one has been separated. Often described as uniquely Brazilian; central to bossa nova and romantic notions of loss and yearning.

### Futuro (Future)
Rather than yearning for the past, saudade do futuro is directed toward futures imagined in the past—promises of technological progress and human advancement that seemed inevitable in earlier eras but now appear as unattainable, nostalgic objects.

## The 1960s Techno-Utopian Promise

Saudade do futuro emerges from the distinctive utopianism of 1950s-1960s popular music and consumer culture:

### Space-Age Bachelor Pad Music
The 1950s-1960s produced a new genre of easy listening designed to showcase technological advances:

- **Stereophonic recording technology**: Hi-fi recordings; stereo record players; stereo test records designed to demonstrate technological features
- **Modern design utopia**: Emphasis on modern furniture, appliances, home technology as basis for human progress and happiness
- **Space-age imagery**: Futuristic aesthetics; technological optimism; belief in technological solutions to human problems
- **World fairs and retro-futurism**: Century-spanning world fairs promised ultra-modern futures (moon landings, supersonic travel, space colonies, automated homes)

### Musical Exemplars
Composers and performers like **Martin Denny**, **Arthur Lyman**, and others created recordings with titles and artwork evoking technological utopianism. These recordings became archived cultural artifacts.

## [[Pizzicato Five]] and Shibuya-kei as Postmodern Saudade do Futuro

[[Roberts]] argues Pizzicato Five and Shibuya-kei artists engage in saudade do futuro through:

### The "New Stereophonic Sound Spectacular" Sample
Pizzicato Five's songs repeatedly sample the phrase "a new stereophonic sound spectacular"—taken from 1960s stereo test records. This sample functions as "an auditory symbol of the techno-futurism of its time":

- **Archaeological reference**: Treating 1960s stereo technology as historical artifact; making audible the technological promise-making of that era
- **Ironic nostalgia**: The sample evokes nostalgia for promises of technological progress now understood as unfulfilled or partial
- **Affect register**: Evoking wistful awareness that this promised future (space colonization, technological abundance) did not arrive as imagined

### Visual Aesthetics
Pizzicato Five's artwork is "pervaded by such nostalgia-for-the-future imagery":

- Ultra-modern lounges (1960s futuristic interiors)
- Nomiya Maki posing in space suit for remix compilation cover
- References to space travel, moon landings, Barbarella (1968 film exemplifying space-age utopianism)
- Retro-futuristic fashion and design
- French Nouvelle Vague cinema (modern, aspirational aesthetics)

### Affective Register: Happy-Sad
This nostalgia for the future produces the characteristic [[happy-sad tonality|happy-sad ambivalence]] of Shibuya-kei:

- **Joy**: Genuine pleasure in 1960s aesthetic vision and technological imaginary; celebration of modernist optimism
- **Sadness/Wistfulness**: Awareness that this future never arrived; knowledge that current present is "much less rosy"; gap between utopian promise and lived reality
- **Ironic pastiche**: Postmodern stance of ironic quotation; simultaneously sincere and skeptical about techno-utopian promise

## Parallels: [[Saudade]] and [[Mono no Aware]]

Roberts notes affective parallels across cultures:

### Saudade (Portuguese/Brazilian)
Profound, bittersweet longing for something lost or unreachable. Central to Brazilian music, particularly bossa nova. Often characterized as uniquely Brazilian.

### Mono no Aware (Japanese)
Often translated as "the pathos of things" or "the sadness of things." Refers to awareness of transience of everything and gentle sadness or wistfulness this brings. Aesthetic sensibility in Japanese art and literature; awareness of impermanence and inevitable loss.

### Shibuya-kei as Bridge
Shibuya-kei, with its incorporation of bossa nova and Japanese aesthetic sensibilities, bridges both affects. Saudade do futuro partakes of both: **bittersweet longing + awareness of transience**.

### Momus's "The Sadness of Things" (1995)
British cultural critic/musician Nick Currie (Momus), resident in Japan during 1990s and collaborator with Shibuya-kei artists, wrote song "The Sadness of Things" (1995) explicitly engaging mono no aware during his involvement with Shibuya-kei scene:

> Mono no aware / The sadness of things  
> Mono no aware / The temptation to see  
> The world as it ought to be  
> Mono no aware / The sadness of things  
> Blinded with tears I can still see  
> My insignificance in an indifferent universe

This self-reflexive engagement with Japanese aesthetic concepts by Western artist embedded in Shibuya-kei scene exemplifies transnational affective circulation.

## Historical Context: Bubble Economy

Saudade do futuro in Shibuya-kei relates to specific historical moment:

- **Late 1980s-early 1990s**: Japanese bubble economy peak; consumer affluence; "Lost Decade" had not yet begun
- **Economic optimism**: Shibuya-kei's "sunniness" reflects pre-crash economic confidence; affluent cosmopolitan urban lifestyle celebration
- **Temporal awareness**: Even in moment of optimism, artists engaged with nostalgic futures; awareness of transience and loss
- **Post-2000 shifts**: As economic stagnation set in, nostalgia for 1990s bubble-era optimism intensified

## Pizzicato One: Post-Earthquake Inflection (2011)

[[Konishi Yasuharu]]'s post-Pizzicato Five project **Pizzicato One**, released May 25, 2011 (10th anniversary of band's dissolution), provides striking example of saudade do futuro's evolution:

### Album Description
Described as collection of "very sad" cover songs; cover depicts black-clad, snow-covered Konishi standing alone in wintry landscape.

### Song Selection
- John Lennon's "Imagine": Quintessential statement of musical utopianism; postmodern quotation
- Brazilian songs including Marcos Valle's "Preciso Aprender a Ser Só" ("I need to learn to be alone", 1965): Portuguese saudade inflected with loneliness and separation

### Post-Tōhoku Resonance
Released in aftermath of March 11, 2011 Tōhoku earthquake and ongoing nuclear crisis:

> While the release of the album in the aftermath of the Tōhoku earthquake and in the midst of Japan's nuclear crisis was no doubt coincidental, its melancholy evocation of the transience of things suddenly became a metaphor for the national mood more generally. In post-311 Japan, its [[mono no aware]] seems as in tune with its time as the music of Pizzicato Five was in the heady days of the bubble economy.

Saudade do futuro—nostalgia for imagined futures—becomes national affect in moment of catastrophe and collective loss.

## Cultural-Historical Shifts

### 1980s-1990s: Techno-Utopian Nostalgia
Shibuya-kei engages saudade do futuro through postmodern ironic quotation of 1960s space-age aesthetics; affection mixed with irony; awareness that technological promise did not materialize as imagined.

### 2000s: Millennium Fatigue
By early 2000s, Y2K fears, dot-com crash, and post-bubble Japan shift affect landscape.

### Post-2011: Catastrophic Loss
Tōhoku earthquake and nuclear crisis transform saudade do futuro from ironic postmodern affect to national metaphor for genuine loss, transience, and uncertainty about futures.

## Theoretical Implications

Saudade do futuro challenges linear time:

- Not simply nostalgia for past (backward-looking)
- Not simply anticipation of future (forward-looking)
- **Paradoxical affect**: Yearning for a future imagined in the past; loss of futures that never arrived
- **Postmodern temporality**: Collapse of past/present/future boundaries; simultaneous inhabitation of multiple temporal regimes
- **Affective circulation**: Transnational movement of concepts ([[saudade]], [[mono no aware]]) produces hybrid affects in Shibuya-kei
