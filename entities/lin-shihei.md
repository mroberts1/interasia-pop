## Overview

Lin Shihei is the most renowned contemporary editor at [[Shueisha]] and current editor of Jump+ (the digital platform of [[Weekly Shōnen Jump]]). With 17+ years of editorial experience, he is credited with introducing global mega-hits including "Chainsaw Man" and "Spy x Family" to the world.

## Career Highlights

### Experience & Tenure

- **Experience:** 17+ years as [[Weekly Shōnen Jump]] / Jump+ editor
- **Current Role:** Editor, Jump+ (Shonen Jump Plus, digital platform)
- **Impact:** Recognized as perhaps the most successful hit-maker in contemporary manga editing

### Major Discoveries & Mentorships

1. **Tatsuki Fujimoto (Chainsaw Man)**
   - Mentored from age 17
   - 7-year development period before Jump+ serialization debut
   - Lin volunteered to manage Fujimoto's early submissions through annual awards
   - Fujimoto sent new works monthly throughout development phase
   - Result: One of the most acclaimed contemporary manga series

2. **Spy x Family** (Author: Tatsuya Endo)
   - Introduction to global audiences
   - Commercial and critical success

### Workload & Scope

**As of 2020**, Lin's active portfolio included:
- **~100 mangaka** under various stages of mentorship (from novices to established veterans)
- **7 active serializations** in Shōnen Jump / Jump+
- **55+ one-shot submissions** reviewed annually
- **~300+ editorial sessions** annually (52 weeks × 5–6 series)

**Editorial Practice:** Maintains regular communication with non-serialized artists, providing guidance on developing works, even if not immediately suitable for serialization.

## Editorial Philosophy

### No Secret Formula

Lin explicitly rejects the notion of a secret formula for hit manga. Instead, he emphasizes:
- **Luck as a factor:** Success partly dependent on unpredictable market response
- **Volume & iteration:** High number of submissions and attempts increases probability of hits
- **Long-term thinking:** Job security at [[Shueisha]] affords luxury of sustained investment in promising but unproven artists

### Industry Saying

Lin illustrates the asymmetry between editor security and artist precarity through the sardonic observation: **"A seasoned [[Weekly Shōnen Jump]] editor stands on the corpses of tens or hundreds of mangaka"** — highlighting how his successes depend partly on artists whose series are canceled.

### Mentorship as Cultural Bridge

Lin actively introduces mangaka to contemporary culture:
- Sends films and books to artists regularly
- Helps artists stay updated with cultural trends
- Addresses cultural and educational gaps, particularly for self-taught mangaka from modest backgrounds

### Personal Welfare Support

Lin's mentorship extends beyond editorial:
- Monitors eating, sleep, and exercise routines of artists
- Assisted in housing searches for newly successful mangaka
- Acts as contract guarantor for emerging artists
- Engages in logistics support (e.g., picking up drafts from distant studios via late-night trains)

## Hiring Path & Motivation

### Shukatsu Entry

- Hired through [[Shukatsu]] process into [[Shueisha]]
- Elite university graduate (like most editors)
- No indicated passion for manga prior to assignment

### Salary-Driven Motivation (Initial)

- Openly acknowledged receiving **multiple job offers** from companies in manufacturing and insurance sectors
- **Chose Shueisha specifically for its higher salary**, not inherent passion for manga
- Reflects broader pattern: Most shukatsu participants prioritize compensation and job security over creative interest

### Career Evolution

Despite initial motivation disconnected from manga passion, Lin evolved into one of the industry's most acclaimed and thoughtful editors, suggesting that:
- Structured environment and job security enabled cultivation of editorial expertise
- Long-term engagement transformed external motivation into genuine professional excellence
- "Interesting work" (rather than romantic "creativity") became defining driver

## Influence & Recognition

### Cultural Status

- Subject of extensive interviews and media coverage
- Frequently cited in Japanese manga industry publications
- Represents contemporary ideal of [[Manga Editor]] within Japan

### Industry Lore

- Contributes to understanding of what constitutes commercially viable manga
- Mentorship model viewed as exemplary within [[Shueisha]]
- Demonstrates potential for editors lacking prior manga passion to achieve exceptional artistic judgments

## Global Impact

Lin's editorial vision directly shapes:
- International circulation of contemporary manga
- Adaptation of Japanese manga into anime, films, and global fandoms
- Positioning of [[Weekly Shōnen Jump]] as cultural export engine for Japan
- Transnational audiences in Asia and beyond

## Comparison to Historical Editors

### Modern vs. Classical

- **Torishima (historical):** Analytical rigor; rejection of 500+ pages; methodical approach
- **Lin (contemporary):** Volume-based discovery; cultural mentorship; long-term artist development
- **Both:** Exemplify "uncreativity" concept; methodical guidance balancing artist creativity

## Connections to Inter-Asia Pop

Lin's editorial work is central to [[Shueisha]]'s role in:
- Manga franchise development for global circulation
- Regional adaptation and localization across Asia
- Transnational fandom engagement
- Japanese soft power in East Asia and beyond
