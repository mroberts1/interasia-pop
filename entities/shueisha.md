## Overview

Shueisha Inc. is one of Japan's three largest publishing companies and a major force in global creative industries. Founded in 1926, it is best known for publishing Weekly Shōnen Jump, the world's best-selling manga magazine and a cultural institution in Japan.

## Key Facts

- **Founded:** 1926
- **Headquarters:** Tokyo (sole office location; no domestic or international branches)
- **Employees:** Over 700 (as of 2021)
- **Revenue:** Over 200 billion yen (~$1.8 billion USD, 2021)
- **Global Ranking:** ~15th largest publisher in the world
- **Market Share:** Shueisha, Kodansha, and Shogakukan collectively account for 4.5% of Japan's publishing workforce but 37% of the publishing market (as of 2018)

## Business Profile

Shueisha publishes a comprehensive range of media:
- Manga magazines and series
- Picture books and light novels
- Educational and reference books
- Novels and other printed media

The company is part of the "Big Three" Japanese publishers alongside Kodansha (est. 1909) and Shogakukan (est. 1922), which have fended off Western competition for decades.

## Global Media Franchises

Several of the world's highest-grossing media franchises originated as manga in Weekly Shōnen Jump:
- **Dragon Ball** ($24 billion)
- **One Piece** ($21 billion)
- **Fist of the North Star** ($21 billion)
- **Yu-Gi-Oh** ($19 billion)

These franchises have expanded into anime, films, video games, merchandise, and theme parks.

## Employment Model

### Lifetime Employment System

Shueisha maintains a traditional Japanese "membership-type" employment system, providing:
- Lifetime employment
- Seniority-based salary and promotion
- Age-based bonuses
- Company benefits exceeding those at major banks and manufacturing giants like Toyota and Sony

This model stands in stark contrast to Western creative industries, where precarity is endemic.

### Recruitment through Shukatsu

- Exclusively recruits elite university graduates through Japan's structured shukatsu (job-hunting) process
- Entry point: ~20 new employees annually
- Highly competitive: ~200 applicants per position
- Candidates aged 25 or younger at hire
- Historically skewed towards University of Tokyo and Waseda University
- Mid-career recruitment is minimal; regular, lifelong hire is the norm

### Non-Job-Specific Hiring (Ikkatsu Saiyō)

Shueisha does not hire for designated roles. After training, new hires are assigned to departments matching demonstrated skills during an onboarding period.

### Compensation

- Young employees (20s): up to 10 million yen annually (~$93,000)
- Significantly higher than 95% of Japanese workers
- Last 8 CEOs earned ~50 million yen annually (vs. ~$10 million for Western media CEOs like Condé Nast's Roger Lynch)

## Creative Culture

Despite elite hiring and lifetime employment, Shueisha demonstrates several counterintuitive features:

1. **Paradox of Uncreativity:** Editors often lack passion for manga or prior relevant experience, yet produce globally influential creative works.
2. **"Last Paradise" for Creative Managers:** Job security allows long-term editorial vision and mentorship, decoupling creativity from precarity.
3. **Internal Promotion:** All 11 Weekly Shōnen Jump editors-in-chief have been promoted internally; none recruited from outside.
4. **Lack of Diversity:** Most senior editors attend elite universities and lack backgrounds in creative arts.

## Regional & Transnational Impact

Shueisha's manga and franchises have become integral to Asian popular culture circulation, particularly in:
- East Asia (Korea, China, Taiwan)
- Southeast Asia
- Global diaspora communities

[[Weekly Shōnen Jump]] remains the primary driver of Shueisha's international influence.
