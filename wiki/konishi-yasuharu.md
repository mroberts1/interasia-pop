# Konishi Yasuharu

**Konishi Yasuharu** is a Japanese music collector, bandleader, and record label founder regarded as one of the most influential figures in the emergence of [[shibuya-kei]]. As founder of [[pizzicato-five]] and the independent label [[Readymade]], Konishi exemplifies the music collector ethos at the heart of the Shibuya-kei movement.

## Background and Music Collecting

Konishi is characterized first and foremost as an **avid music collector**—a "record collector first and a musician second," according to cultural critic W. David Marx. This collector identity fundamentally shaped Shibuya-kei's aesthetic:

- **Obsessive curation**: Spent decades acquiring obscure recordings from Japanese and Western pop traditions
- **Economic privilege**: Upper-middle-class background; supported by family to pursue collecting and musical production; reportedly spent family money on records until age 30
- **Taste-making**: Engaged in connoisseurship; identification of "most obscure (and therefore, best)" records
- **Reintroduction**: Like DJ Shadow school of hip hop, Shibuya-kei project was about "finding and buying the most obscure (and therefore, best) records and reintroducing them to the world"

## Pizzicato Five: Iconic Band

Konishi founded [[pizzicato-five]] with frontwoman [[pizzicato-five]]. The band became the most iconic exemplar of Shibuya-kei through:

### Musical Method
- **Sampling and pastiche**: Reintroduction of pre-existing media (1960s easy listening, Motown, bossa nova, exotica, French pop, disco, club music)
- **Orchestral sophistication**: Complex arrangements; strings and horns; cinematic production
- **Lifestyle lyrics**: Focus on shopping, nightlife, fashion, romance, café culture; leisure and play as valid subjects

### Visual Identity
Konishi oversaw visual aesthetics emphasizing 1960s retro-futurism and French Nouvelle Vague references. Nomiya Maki posed as 1960s fashion icons; album art evoked ultra-modern lounges and space-age imagery.

### Production Credits
- Worked with key Shibuya-kei collaborators and producers
- Managed band's sonic and aesthetic evolution across multiple albums and remix compilations
- Pioneered sampling and pastiche techniques in Japanese pop context

### Commercial Success
Pizzicato Five became first Japanese pop band to gain wide international attention among Western youth (US and Europe). Band performed at New York New Music Seminar (1992-1994); albums distributed by Matador Records (US); reportedly sold over 100,000 records per artist for the label.

## Readymade Records: Independent Label

Konishi founded **Readymade** as distribution platform for Shibuya-kei artists. The label was instrumental in circulating independent music during movement's growth:

- **Artist roster**: Pizzicato Five, Love Tambourines, Kahimi Karie, Takako Minegawa, Yukari Fresh, Bridge, and numerous others
- **International expansion**: Readymade International distributed material to Europe and North America
- **Indie infrastructure**: Part of broader network of Japanese indie labels ([[Crue-L]], [[Trattoria]], [[Escalator]]) that anchored Shibuya-kei scene
- **Long-term operations**: Continued releasing material through 2000s-2010s; archive of Shibuya-kei and related artists

## Aesthetic Philosophy

Konishi's approach to music-making reflects broader Shibuya-kei principles:

### Connoisseurship Through Consumption
- **Taste as identity**: Consumer choice as marker of refined aesthetics and cultural capital
- **Anti-mainstream stance**: Shibuya-kei "exclaimed, you are all consuming the wrong goods!" against mainstream J-pop
- **Educated audience**: Target audience were "upper middle class educated kids" with similar taste-making practices
- **Market response**: The market (record labels, retailers) subsequently provided "sophisticated goods" and incorporated Shibuya-kei into mainstream middle-class lifestyle

### Internationalism
- **Aesthetic eclecticism**: Drawing from 1960s French pop, British indie, American Motown/easy listening, Brazilian bossa nova, European film soundtracks
- **Deliberate cosmopolitanism**: Against provincial or nationalist aesthetic; embrace of Western and global influences
- **Record store culture**: Enabled by Shibuya district's ecosystem of HMV, Tower Records, Wave, Shinseido; access to imported foreign music crucial
- **Transnational networks**: Collaborations with British (él Records), French, American, Brazilian musicians

### Happy-Sad Tonality
Pizzicato Five's characteristic emotional ambivalence—joy tinged with nostalgia and wistfulness—reflects Konishi's sensibility:

- Engagement with 1960s technological utopianism; nostalgia for promised futures
- Awareness of transience and impermanence ([[mono no aware]])
- Simultaneous celebration of consumer lifestyle and melancholic recognition of its limitations

## Collaborations and Influence

### British Connection
Collaborated with [[Philippe Auclair]] (Louis Philippe), él Records alumnus. Oyamada's Trattoria label released compilation _Fab Gear_ (1991) featuring Auclair and él Records alumni; this compilation is mentioned by [[Nick Currie]] (Momus) as "the founding record, some say, of Shibuya-kei."

### Production Work
- Produced early material for [[Kahimi Karie]] and other Shibuya-kei artists
- Worked with international producers and musicians on remixes and collaborations

### Transnational Soundscape
Konishi exemplifies [[transnational soundscape]] concept—his work cannot be understood as purely Japanese phenomenon but as participation in international networks of musicians, labels, and audiences spanning London, Paris, New York, Rio, Berlin.

## Solo Projects and Later Work

Following Pizzicato Five's official dissolution in 2001:

### Pizzicato One (2011)
Released May 25, 2011 (10th anniversary of band's dissolution). Described as collection of "very sad" cover songs:

- Recorded as solo/collaborative project with various singers performing covers in English
- Selection emphasizes loss, transience, and melancholic beauty
- Songs include John Lennon's "Imagine" (quintessential musical utopianism); Marcos Valle's "Preciso Aprender a Ser Só" ("I need to learn to be alone", 1965; emphasizes saudade of Portuguese original)
- Released in aftermath of Tōhoku earthquake; [[mono no aware]] resonates with post-disaster national mood
- Cover imagery: Black-clad, snow-covered Konishi standing alone in wintry landscape

### Enduring Influence
Konishi remains revered figure within Japanese music industry; founding members of Pizzicato Five continue to be influential.

## Cultural Significance

Konishi Yasuharu represents:

- **Record collector as cultural authority**: Music knowledge and taste-making through curation and connoisseurship
- **Subculture producer**: Creation of aesthetic stance against mainstream while eventually achieving mainstream success
- **Internationalist vision**: Embracing global influences; participation in transnational music networks
- **Happy-sad sensibility**: Emotional ambivalence; simultaneous celebration and melancholic awareness
- **Nostalgic futurism**: Engagement with 1960s technological utopianism through [[saudade do futuro|"nostalgia for the future"]] lens
