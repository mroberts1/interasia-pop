## Persons

### Artists & musicians

- [[ymo]] — Yellow Magic Orchestra. Pioneering synth-pop/techno-pop trio (1978–1983); cultural icons of postmodern Japan; self-orientalisation as strategy
- [[ryuichi-sakamoto]] — YMO keyboardist; experimental composer; film-scoring pioneer; classical training
- [[pizzicato-five]] — Flagship Shibuya-kei band; postmodern pastiche of 1960s easy listening, Motown, bossa nova; Konishi + Nomiya Maki as vocalist
- [[konishi-yasuharu]] — Music collector; founder of Pizzicato Five and Readymade Records; seminal Shibuya-kei figure

### Editors & industry

- [[kazuhiko-torishima]] — Legendary manga editor; mentored Akira Toriyama; exemplifies editorial rigor
- [[lin-shihei]] — Contemporary Shueisha editor; discovered Chainsaw Man and Spy x Family

### Publishers & labels

- [[shueisha]] — Japan's third-largest publisher; home of Weekly Shōnen Jump; $1.8B revenue
- [[weekly-shonen-jump]] — World's best-selling manga magazine; home of Dragon Ball, One Piece, Chainsaw Man

## Concepts

### Aesthetics & movements

- [[shibuya-kei]] — Independent J-pop movement (late 1980s–1990s); eclectic internationalism; transnational soundscape; happy-sad affect
- [[postmodern-japan]] — Political-to-cultural shift 1978–1985; hybridity, pastiche, irony; YMO as epitome; pre-bubble-economy era
- [[transnational-soundscape]] — Framework for understanding Shibuya-kei as international co-production; music not bound by national categories
- [[saudade-do-futuro]] — "Nostalgia for the future"; Portuguese concept; yearning for 1960s techno-utopian promises; core affect of Shibuya-kei
- [[techno-orientalism]] — Postmodern Orientalism; East Asia as hyper-modern/emotionless/threatening; self-orientalisation as strategic response

### Industries & labor

- [[manga-industry-japan]] — Big Three publishers; "mother content" model; 37% of Japanese publishing market
- [[manga-editor]] — Creative manager role; permanent employment; bridges executives and mangaka
- [[creative-labor-japan]] — Membership-type employment; lifetime security for managers, precarity for creators; decoupling of creativity from risk
- [[shukatsu]] — Formalised Japanese graduate recruitment; elite university graduates; 200:1 competition at top publishers

## Research Threads

- **YMO as decolonial move.** Mōri's argument that YMO's self-orientalisation is critical engagement rather than accommodation is the vault's most contentious thread. Needs corroboration from independent YMO scholars (or a second source genuinely disagreeing, which would be valuable).
- **Transnational soundscape as theoretical frame.** Roberts's Shibuya-kei analysis treats the music as international co-production. Extending this to other Asian pop phenomena (K-pop's K-hip-hop-via-Atlanta; Hong Kong's Cantopop absorbing Motown) would be a high-leverage thread.
- **Creative-labor asymmetry.** Oyama's decoupling of creative managers (secure) from creators (precarious) reframes the Western "creative class" thesis. Needs comparison pages with K-pop idol-system labor and Hong Kong film-industry labor to become properly transnational.
