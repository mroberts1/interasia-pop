# Pizzicato Five

**Pizzicato Five** is a Japanese indie pop band and the most iconic exemplar of the [[shibuya-kei]] movement. Fronted by the glamorous vocalist [[pizzicato-five]], the band defined the aesthetic and emotional signature of 1990s Japanese independent music through postmodern pastiche of jazz, easy listening, Motown, disco, and club music.

## Formation and Key Members

The band was founded by **Konishi Yasuharu** (music collector, bandleader) and featured:

- **Nomiya Maki** (vocals): The face and voice of Pizzicato Five; iconic glamorous figure embodying Shibuya-kei aesthetics
- **Konishi Yasuharu** (keyboards, bandleader)
- Other members varied across releases and lineup changes

## Musical Style

Pizzicato Five's sound is characterized by:

- **Postmodern eclecticism**: Fusion of 1960s easy listening, Motown, disco, club music, jazz, bossa nova
- **Sample-heavy production**: Pastiche and reintroduction of obscure pre-existing recordings
- **Orchestral arrangements**: Sophisticated, cinematic production; strings and horn sections
- **Lyrical focus**: Leisure, shopping, nightlife, fashion, romance, café culture; celebration of consumer lifestyle
- **Happy-sad tonality**: Joy and whimsy juxtaposed with melancholy and nostalgia; [[happy sad|"Happy Sad"]] EP exemplifies this emotional signature

## Visual Aesthetics

Pizzicato Five's visual identity was as important as its music:

- **1960s references**: Nomiya Maki posed as 1960s fashion icons (Twiggy, Veruschka); album art evoked French nouvelle vague (Jacques Demy, Michel Legrand, Fellini's _La Dolce Vita_)
- **Retro-futurism**: Space-age imagery; Nomiya in space suit; ultra-modern lounges; nostalgia for 1960s techno-utopian future
- **Cinematic influences**: Early 1985 photograph against Jacques Tati's _Mon Oncle_ film poster (purchased in Paris); _Audrey Hepburn Complex_ single title
- **French glamour**: Appropriation of Nouvelle Vague aesthetics, 1960s fashion, European nobility

## Discography Highlights

- **First single**: "Audrey Hepburn Complex" (1985), produced by [[Hosono Haruomi]]
- **Key albums**: Mix of studio releases and remix compilations; emphasis on reinterpretation and pastiche
- **_Happy Sad_** (EP): Title exemplifies band's emotional signature
- **_Happy End of the World_** (1997 album): Dissonant title recalling [[Hosono Haruomi]]'s 1970s pre-YMO band Happy End
- **Continuous evolution**: Compilations, remixes, stylistic tweakings throughout career

## Commercial Success and International Reach

- **Domestic**: Revered and influential figures within mainstream Japanese music industry by 2000s
- **International**: First Japanese pop band to gain wide international attention among youth in US and Europe
- **US distribution**: Distributed by [[Matador Records]]; reportedly sold over 100,000 records for the label
- **New York debut**: Performed at New York New Music Seminar (1992-1994)
- **Film/media**: _Happy Sad_ EP featured in Robert Altman's 1995 documentary _Unzipped_ about fashion designer Isaac Mizrahi
- **Club remixes**: From mid-1990s onwards, songs became popular sources for remixes by DJs in Europe and US

## Transnational Connections

Pizzicato Five exemplifies [[shibuya-kei]] as [[transnational soundscape]]:

- **French influences**: Ostentatious Francophilia; [[Kahimi Karie]] connection; samples of French chanson; appropriation of Nouvelle Vague
- **UK indie lineage**: Influenced by British label [[él Records]]; aesthetic parallels with art-pop's neo-baroque stylization
- **Brazilian bossa nova**: Incorporation of bossa nova into eclectic mix; participation in transnational easy-listening revival
- **Global indie circuit**: Performances and releases across London, Paris, Berlin, New York, Latin America

## Affect: Happy-Sad and [[saudade-do-futuro]]

Pizzicato Five's music is characterized by **happy-sad ambivalence**—simultaneous joy and melancholy. This reflects:

- **Nostalgia for the 1960s**: Particularly for that era's naive optimism about technological progress and modern design
- **[[Saudade do futuro]]**: Portuguese concept of "nostalgia for the future"; yearning for the space-age promise of the 1960s (moon landings, supersonic travel, Barbarella-style futures) now experienced as ironic through 1990s postmodern lens
- **Commodity culture utopia**: Celebration of consumer lifestyle and refined taste; leisure over labor
- **Bubble economy era**: Reflects pre-2000 Japanese economic optimism; affluent, cosmopolitan urban aesthetics

The band's visual and sonic aesthetic embodies a fantasy of ultra-modern futures that never arrived, now archived as objects of wistful, ironic nostalgia.

## Collaborations and Influence

- **Studio collaborations**: Worked with Western musicians and labels throughout career
- **Remixes**: Extensively remixed by [[Towa Tei]] and other DJs
- **Later projects**: Members pursued solo and collaborative work post-official dissolution

## Band Dissolution and Legacy

- **Official dissolution**: 2001 (10th anniversary marked in 2011)
- **Pizzicato One** (2011): Post-dissolution project by Konishi Yasuharu, released on May 25, 2011 (10th anniversary date). Described as collection of "very sad" cover songs including John Lennon's "Imagine" and Marcos Valle's "Preciso Aprender a Ser Só." Album released post-Tōhoku earthquake; [[mono no aware]] became metaphor for national mood.
- **Revivals**: Tributes, retrospectives, and remixes continue; founding members remain influential figures in Japanese music industry

## Cultural Significance

Pizzicato Five represents:

- **First international J-pop success**: Pioneered global attention to Japanese popular music among Western youth
- **Postmodern aesthetic exemplar**: Exemplified 1990s postmodern pastiche, retromania, eclectic internationalism in popular music
- **Happy-sad emotional signature**: Established emotional tonality (joy + nostalgia) that became characteristic of Shibuya-kei
- **Subculture to mainstream**: Defined themselves aesthetically against mainstream J-pop; gradually incorporated into mainstream while retaining subcultural reverence
- **Consumer culture aestheticization**: Made lifestyle consumption and refinement of taste into artistic statement; leisure and play as valid subjects for pop music
